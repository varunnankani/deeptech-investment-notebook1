# PierSight - Working Investment Notes

## One-line thesis
PierSight is not attractive because SAR is novel. The bet is that a maritime-specific, lower-cost observation architecture can create persistent vessel data that is monetised repeatedly as security, underwriting, sanctions/compliance and trading intelligence.

## White space
Maritime security often relies on AIS (which can be disabled/spoofed), optical imagery (cloud/night constraints) and reactive high-resolution tasking. PierSight is trying to optimise for persistent, wide-area awareness rather than only point imagery.

## What must be true
- The maritime-specific architecture produces a material cost / coverage / latency advantage.
- MATSYA becomes a real intelligence product rather than a viewer on top of imagery.
- The same observations can support multiple paid workflows.
- Recurring analytics revenue grows faster than satellite count / capex.

## Competition
ICEYE and Capella validate SAR-based maritime surveillance. RF-intelligence players such as HawkEye 360 / Unseenlabs attack parts of the same dark-vessel problem from a different sensor layer.

## End-state
**“Bloomberg for the ocean”** - one intelligence layer used by coast guards, insurers, banks/compliance teams, traders and maritime operators.

## Kill criteria
- Revenue remains mostly per-image/tasking/coverage.
- MATSYA creates little measurable value above commoditised data.
- Owning satellites does not improve latency, accuracy, economics or data history enough to justify capex.

## Venture-scale metric
**Recurring analytics revenue per satellite** should rise over time.
