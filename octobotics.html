ASSET HEALTH INTELLIGENCE / INVESTMENT DEEP DIVE

Octobotics

Robotic inspection and intelligence for critical infrastructure.

My investment case starts with an existing mandatory inspection budget, not with a speculative new market. The question is whether robotics can create enough operating leverage to turn field services into an asset-integrity platform.

PAIN-POINT & CAPABILITY

When access is difficult, the inspection budget already exists.

Refineries, storage tanks, ships, pipelines, pressure vessels and rail assets already require inspection. Much of that workflow can still depend on scaffolding, work at height, shutdown windows and skilled manual NDT. Octobotics does not need to invent demand. It needs to prove a safer and more efficient way to spend an existing budget.

01

Inspect

Robotic platforms access difficult surfaces and capture visual, thickness, corrosion and weld data.

02

Analyse

Inspection data becomes corrosion maps, defect findings, condition trends and risk indicators.

03

Act

The higher-value layer is decision support around maintenance, shutdown prioritisation and asset life.

THE UNDERWRITING
01 / CURRENT OPERATIONS

Services-first lowers adoption friction

Customers can buy an inspection outcome rather than make an upfront robotics capex decision. That makes the field-service model a distribution wedge rather than automatically a weakness.

02 / WHITE SPACE

Between NDT service firms and robotics companies

The opportunity sits where inspection domain expertise, reliable robotics, certification and industrial trust have to work together. A crawler alone is not the product.

03 / WHY NOW

The need is not new. The economic crossover is.

Better sensing, edge compute and autonomy improve field reliability while safety, downtime and specialist-labour costs raise the cost of manual access.

04 / WHAT MUST BE TRUE

Platform reuse has to beat custom engineering

New verticals should reuse a meaningful share of the hardware, autonomy and software stack. Otherwise expansion can become several small engineering businesses under one brand.

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Competition validates the category and sets the clock.

PetroBot is the closest domestic head-to-head. Gecko Robotics is the global scaled benchmark. Solinas is an adjacent Indian robotics threat. The investment case survives only if Octobotics develops commercial differentiation beyond simply being local.

THE SINGLE METRICRevenue per field engineer

If this rises alongside inspections per robot and contribution margin per job, the robotics layer is creating operating leverage. If revenue remains closely correlated with field-team growth, the business remains linear.

END-STATE

Not primarily a robot manufacturer.

I see the end-state as an asset-integrity platform for critical infrastructure. Robotic inspection is the collection layer. Repeated inspection creates condition history. The higher-value product can eventually become understanding how an asset is degrading, where intervention is needed and potentially what is likely to fail next.

I do not need that software layer to exist today for the investment to work, but I do need evidence that operational leverage is improving.

FOUNDER ROOM / 30 MINUTES

Questions designed to change the investment decision.

01

Walk me through the last ten inspection jobs, including price, fully loaded field cost and contribution margin.

02

When you enter a new asset class, what percentage of hardware, autonomy and software is reused?

03

Show me the dated path to hazardous-area certification and the customer pipeline it unlocks.

04

How are inspections per robot and revenue per field engineer changing quarter by quarter?

05

What rights does Octobotics retain over inspection data, and what are customers actually willing to let you reuse?

06

Where does the model stop being a services business and start becoming an asset-intelligence platform?
