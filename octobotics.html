OCTOBOTICS / ROBOTIC NDT

From robotic NDT to asset intelligence.

My investment case starts with an existing mandatory inspection budget, not a speculative new market. The question is whether robotics can create enough operating leverage to turn field services into an asset-integrity platform.

The underwriting mechanism

The category is attractive only if field deployment compounds into reusable capability, better economics and eventually decision intelligence.

01 / CURRENT OPERATIONS

Services-first as distribution

Customers buy an inspection outcome rather than unfamiliar robotics capex.

02 / WHITE SPACE

Between NDT and robotics

Inspection expertise, certification, reliable robotics and industrial trust have to work together.

03 / WHY NOW

The economic crossover

Better sensing and autonomy meet rising safety, shutdown and specialist-labour costs.

04 / WHAT MUST BE TRUE

Reuse beats custom engineering

New verticals must reuse enough hardware, autonomy and software to prevent linear custom work.

05 / KILL CRITERION

Revenue cannot track headcount

If growth needs roughly proportional field teams and robots, the venture-scale case weakens.

Competition validates the category and sets the clock.

Octobotics still has to prove differentiation beyond being local. PetroBot, Gecko Robotics, Solinas and traditional NDT providers each attack part of the opportunity.

PetroBot

Closest Indian head-to-head. Certification progress and strategic oil and gas backing raise the benchmark.

Gecko Robotics

Scaled global benchmark for robotic inspection and asset intelligence.

Solinas

Adjacent Indian inspection robotics player with the potential to converge on overlapping use cases.

Traditional NDT / TIC

Trusted incumbents already own inspection budgets and customer relationships.

From inspection to intelligence

The first leverage can come from robotics itself. The longer-term upside is a compounding asset-condition history that supports higher-value decisions.

If this rises alongside inspections per robot and contribution margin per job, the robotics layer is creating operating leverage. If revenue remains closely correlated with field-team growth, the business remains linear.

Questions I would ask the founding team before investing

Each question should be capable of changing the investment decision, not simply filling an information gap.

01

Walk me through the last ten inspection jobs, including price, fully loaded field cost and contribution margin.

02

When you enter a new asset class, what percentage of hardware, autonomy and software is reused?

03

Show me the dated path to hazardous-area certification and the customer pipeline it unlocks.

04

How are inspections per robot and revenue per field engineer changing quarter by quarter?

05

What rights does Octobotics retain over inspection data, and what are customers actually willing to let you reuse?

06

Where does the model stop being a services business and start becoming an asset-intelligence platform?
