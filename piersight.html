// THE UNDERWRITING

01

White space

Persistent and affordable maritime surveillance is still a hard systems problem. The opportunity is not simply better imagery. It is reducing the cost and latency of keeping watch over large ocean areas.

02

Why now

Private space infrastructure, smaller satellites, commercial launch access and Indian policy changes create room for mission-specific constellations that historically required state-level budgets.

03

What must be true

The maritime architecture must provide a measurable cost, persistence or workflow advantage. MATSYA must create intelligence value that customers will pay for repeatedly.

04

What I will not assume

Sovereignty is a procurement and positioning advantage, not a protected market. Global competitors can and do sell into the region.

If intelligence revenue grows faster than satellite count, the observation layer is being monetised repeatedly. If every incremental dollar requires similar new satellite capex, the model remains capacity-bound.

// END-STATE

"Bloomberg Terminal for the ocean"

The vision is not another satellite-image marketplace. It is an intelligence layer through which important maritime decisions are made. Persistent observation combines with AIS, external datasets, behavioural history, alerts and APIs.

Government customers can anchor the network. Commercial workflows can create the recurring intelligence flywheel.
